The History Shelf

The past, told well. We curate history the way we curate everything else — pickily. Church history and the Reformers, the ancient and medieval world, America's story, and the wars that shaped it, chosen for writing worth a slow, attentive read.

The shelf runs on two tracks: new & notable titles worth buying now, and a deep bench of used & vintage copies — out-of-print and hard-to-find, one copy each, shipped from Chattanooga.

How we choose

Every book on this shelf is checked before it goes up. We read the publisher's description, trade and reader reviews, and look at the author's earlier work.

We look for

  • Careful scholarship from a trusted author or press
  • A fair hearing for the people and arguments of the past
  • Writing a general reader can enjoy, not only specialists

We leave off

  • Pseudo-history, conspiracy theories, and “secret history” sensationalism
  • Books written mainly to discredit a faith or tradition by misrepresenting it
  • Books whose main subject is the history of sexuality or sexual identity
  • Graphic violence or sexual content included to shock rather than to inform
  • Thin or rushed books, like quick anniversary tie-ins

History is often hard reading. Books on war, slavery, or genocide have to describe terrible things, and we don't hold that against them. When a book's descriptions of atrocity or abuse go well beyond what readers of that subject would expect, we add a content note.

Some histories argue a point of view. We include them when the history underneath is solid, and we say so in the description.

If you read one and think we missed something, write to us at info@thepickyreader.com.
